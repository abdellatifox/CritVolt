Endfield is the rare spin-off that is not trying to be the game it came from.
Arknights was tower defence. This is an action RPG with a factory sim inside it.

## The automation

The factory layer is the reason to be here. Building production chains across the
map is not a side activity bolted to an RPG — it is a system with enough depth that
players compare layouts, and it feeds directly into what you can field and sustain.

Comparisons to Death Stranding's building have been made and they are fair: the
satisfaction is in slowly making a hostile map tractable.

## The combat

Fluid, and more importantly legible. Party-based action games usually lose clarity
as the roster grows; Endfield mostly holds it together, and the industrial sci-fi
art direction does real work here — the screen stays readable because the visual
language is disciplined.

## The economy

This is where the reservations live, and they are the same reservations most critics
landed on. The systems underneath are strong enough that they did not need a gacha
wrapped around them, and the progress gates are placed where you feel them.

None of it is unusually predatory by the standards of the genre. That is not a
defence so much as a description of the standard.

## Who it is for

If factory games are your thing, the automation alone justifies the download —
it is free, and the first stretch is generous. If you bounce off gacha economies
on principle, nothing here will change your mind, because the economy is not a
layer you can ignore.

## Verdict

A genuinely inventive game that keeps bumping into its own business model. The
79 it averaged with critics feels about right: excellent parts, an obvious ceiling.
