Playing as Naoe, this is a stealth game with real teeth. Playing as Yasuke, it is
an action game about walking through the front door. Shadows never fully decides
which one it is, and that is both its most interesting quality and its biggest
structural problem.

## Naoe

The shinobi half is the reason to be here. Light, fast, and built around a
verticality the series has been drifting away from for years. Crucially, the
stealth is failable in a way that matters — detection has consequences beyond a
brief chase.

The prone system and the light-and-shadow model do real work. Blowing out a
lantern is a decision, not a flourish.

## Yasuke

The samurai half is competent and never quite essential. Combat is weighty and
readable, and there is genuine satisfaction in walking into a compound that Naoe
would have to unpick for twenty minutes.

But it is also the half that turns the game back into the open-world checklist
Assassin's Creed has been running for a decade.

## The world

Feudal Japan is a setting the series was always going to reach, and the version
here is unusually restrained. Fewer icons, more looking. The seasons are the
standout: they change traversal and stealth, not just the colour palette.

## Where it strains

The two protagonists rarely need each other. Very few missions are built so that
switching is the answer, which means most players will pick a favourite in the
first five hours and stay there — and half the game quietly becomes optional.

## Verdict

The best stealth Assassin's Creed in years, carrying an action game it does not
need. If you play it mostly as Naoe, it is close to great.
