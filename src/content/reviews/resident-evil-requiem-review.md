The camera toggle got the headlines. It is not why Requiem is good.

## Pacing

Requiem's real achievement is rhythm. It understands that horror is not constant
pressure but the gap between pressures, and it spends that gap well — letting you
think you have a safe room, then quietly taking it away.

## Both cameras

First-person is the more frightening of the two and the more exhausting. Third-person
is the more playable and the more forgiving. Neither is the "correct" mode, and the
game is balanced well enough that both hold up across a full run.

## Resources

Ammunition is tight without being punitive, and the game is honest about it: you
can see what you are spending and what it costs you later.

## Where it sags

The middle act sends you back through cleared space more often than it needs to.
It is the one stretch where the pacing loosens.

## Verdict

A confident, tightly-built survival horror that would still be excellent with one
camera. Having two is a bonus, not the point.
